Math Projects

Hierarchical dependence models for binary data

This research has been supervised by Prof. Nešlehová and Prof. Valiquette. It began as my MATH 470 Honours project, building on recent work on hierarchical "maximum-aggregation" trees for multivariate Bernoulli data. After learning and reviewing the theory, my research focused on learning a tree's unknown structure from data alone. We discovered that the correlation matrix factors into a marginal part and a matrix Θ whose block structure encodes the tree. I designed and tested greedy algorithms in Python to recover the tree using an estimated Θ. The project has since been extended past its original scope, and I have continued the work over the summer with Prof. Valiquette.

Scoring optimization of the Lizard Cult in the asymmetric game Root

This project aims to model the game state of the asymmetric board game Root from the perspective of the Lizard Cult faction, who have a unique scoring method using gardens. I do this using Markov chains, and attempt to derive optimal strategies. I also use Monte Carlo methods to simulate these policies in Python (matplotlib, NumPy). This work appears in the 9th issue of The Delta-Epsilon, McGill's undergraduate mathematics journal.

Proof formalisation in the Lean language

I was part of a Directed Reading Program (DRP) group at McGill along with Gabriella Chen and Eloïse Freydier, guided by (now-Dr.) Jérémie Turcotte. We learned how Lean uses type theory, and taught ourselves how to prove basic facts in Lean (sometimes, the 'easier' stuff is harder than the 'hard' stuff). By the end of the program, we had formalized our own proofs of several results from Analysis 1 & 2. I would highly recommend the DRP to anyone!

Canards in slow-fast dynamical systems

As an Honours student, I had to complete a research project on nonlinear dynamics. I chose the topic of canards, a phenomenon where the tiniest variation of a parameter can cause the dynamics of a system to wildly explode. I also explored some advanced techniques in the study of canards, then summarized all my readings in a report.
